Approach

A pretrained detector and a tracker turn the video into trajectories. A model of the fixed camera, learned without labels from the sample videos, says where the carriageway is, which way each lane flows and where traffic queues. One rule per event class reads trajectories against that model. A separate, strictly causal copy of the perception stack estimates accident risk frame by frame.

sampling

Frames

The camera writes 4K 10-bit video. The decoder skips frames no other frame depends on (2 of every 3), scales the rest to a fixed 1920-pixel working width in C, and runs in a background thread while the GPU detects. Analysis: 8 frames per second on GPU (5 on CPU).

learned

Detect

YOLO11-m at 1280 px, FP16, batches of 8 (YOLO11-n at 640 px on CPU). COCO classes: people, bicycles, cars, motorcycles, buses, trucks, animals, traffic lights.

classical

Track

ByteTrack-style: Kalman filter, high/low-score two-stage matching, class-group gating, a centre-distance fallback for fast objects, and 12 s survival for stationary objects hidden by passing traffic.

classical

Trajectories

Fragments of one object are stitched, people riding a bicycle or motorcycle are not counted as pedestrians, positions are smoothed with a local linear fit. Speed and acceleration are measured in object sizes per second (perspective-robust).

classical

Register the view

The camera is re-aimed between recordings (up to 62 px and 2 % zoom on the samples). The video's background is matched to the reference view with SIFT features and a RANSAC homography; trajectories are mapped into it, so the learned map and the traced zones fit every framing.

learned, no labels

Scene model

From all sample trajectories: carriageway mask, per-cell lane direction, traffic streams, where vehicles wait, stop lines fitted along where the first car of each lane waits (different vehicles that drove in, waited and drove off again, over several signal cycles), movement statistics. The junction's crosswalks are traced once in the reference view; drawn solid lines and no-turn rules can be added the same way.

classical

Pixel side channels

At 1 fps: median background, objects that appear and stay on the road, flickering fire-coloured pixels, grey textureless smoke, traffic-light colour.

rules

Event rules

One module per family of classes, each returning candidate segments with a confidence and the tracks that caused them.

rules

Segments

Per class: merge fragments closer than 1 s, drop blips under 0.8 s, clip to the video. Same-class segments never overlap.

Event rules

Every rule is written against the annotation conventions (when an event starts and ends), because segments are matched by temporal IoU up to 0.7. A class predicted but absent from the test set adds a zero to the macro F1, so each rule prefers precision.

Part B — accident anticipation

RiskEstimator.step receives frames one by one and never opens the video or reads Part A output. Every k-th frame (6 per second on GPU, 3 on CPU) goes through the detector and its own online tracker. Four cues are computed from the current tracks only and fused as 1 − ∏(1 − cue):

Conflict

For closing pairs, time and distance of closest approach under constant velocity. Only head-on geometry counts (predicted miss distance ≤ half a size; passing in the next lane does not). Risk ≈ 0.5 at 1.2 s, rising as contact nears. Far-road boxes and ordinary same-lane following are ignored.

Hard braking

A vehicle losing half its speed within a second with another road user just ahead of it; capped so braking alone never raises an alarm.

Wrong way

A vehicle against the learned lane direction for ≥ 3 s (only when the first frame registers to the reference view).

Impact

Boxes touching while one party stops abruptly: the crash itself, which keeps the alarm from flickering.

An alarm (score ≥ 0.5) needs the fused cues above 0.5 on two consecutive analysed frames; a one-frame spike keeps its rank (0.49) for average precision but raises no alarm. Once raised, the score is held for 1.5 s before decaying, so one near collision gives one alarm rather than a burst.

Engineering

Time budget

The limit is 3× the video length for Part A and Part B together; over it, the harness scores the video as empty. Decoding the camera's 4K 10-bit video is the main cost, so Part A skips non-reference frames and scales in a background thread while the GPU detects 8 fps; Part B detects 6 fps. Both parts pace themselves against one per-video clock and lower their rate if they fall behind. Measured with the unchanged harness on a 4K sample, 4 CPU cores and no GPU: 2.09× real time (Part A 0.83×, Part B 1.26×).

Determinism

Fixed seeds, cuDNN deterministic mode, deterministic frame selection (by timestamp, not wall clock) and a deterministic tracker. Two runs give the same predictions.json.

Caching

Detections and tracks can be cached per video (ROADWATCH_CACHE), so re-scoring after a rule change takes seconds. The official run does not use a cache.

Offline

Weights ship in weights/ (YOLO11-m 40 MB, YOLO11-n 5.6 MB, plus the scene model). Ultralytics is forced offline; nothing is downloaded at run time.
